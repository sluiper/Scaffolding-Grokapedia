# Chapter 10 — Foundations, Ground Conditions & Sole Boards

**Status:** Production draft v0.4  
**Date:** 28 July 2026  

## MCR Mapping

## 1. Load path principle

All scaffold load reaches the ground (or supporting structure) through **standards → base plate/jack → sole board (if used) → bearing surface**. Weak link at the base undermines every upper control.

## 2. Base plate & jack

| Item | Rule | MCR |
|------|------|-----|
| Base plate | 150 × 150 × 6 mm | 003 |
| Jack extension | ≤ **2/3** of thread height | 004 |

Excessive jack extension reduces stability and increases buckling risk [SYNTHESIS — training rule; manufacturer confirm GAP].

## 3. Sole boards

| Rule | Value / note | MCR |
|------|--------------|-----|
| Minimum size (single) | 38 × 225 × 450 mm | 005 |
| Purpose | Spread load to ground | — |
| Omit if | Asphalt or concrete surface | 006 |
| Never | Return sole boards to scaffold-board service | 007 |
| Shared double-standard | 750 × 225 × 75 mm sole **or** two 450 × 225 × 38 mm | 027 |
| Special / load-bearing | Double soles may be used | PPTX s13 |

## 4. Unsafe foundations (bad practice set)

Module-1 s60 flags unsafe foundations (soft ground without adequate spread, sloping/unsecured bases, etc.). **MCR-033.**  
**GAP-016:** Scaffolds near excavations — slide title only; no body text. Until SWP/client rule loaded: **do not place scaffold where excavation can undermine base** without engineering and excavation SWP coordination [SYNTHESIS].

## 5. Worked example — soft ground [SYNTHESIS]

If soil is soft and standards share load, prefer **shared heavy sole** (750×225×75) or engineered spreader. Never rely on small plate alone on soft ground.
