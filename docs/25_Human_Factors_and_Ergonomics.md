## 1. Why human factors belong in a scaffold encyclopedia

Most scaffold harm is not “unknown physics.” It is:

- Using a **red-tagged** or incomplete structure  
- Removing a board “just for access”  
- Climbing with materials (MCR-061)  
- Fatigue / heat / production pressure  
- Unclear who may modify (MCR-060)

[SYNTHESIS] Aligns with FMEA themes in Ch19.

## 2. Error modes (scaffold-specific)

| Mode | Example | Countermeasure |
|------|---------|----------------|
| Violation (routine) | Walk past yellow without harness | Tag discipline MCR-028; supervision |
| Violation (exceptional) | Remove guardrail for lift-in | Engineering sequence + MCR-060 |
| Slip | Miss step on ladder | 4:1 ladder, three-point (MCR-023) |
| Lapse | Forget midrail on hop-up | Handover checklist Ch17 |
| Mistake | Wrong duty class assumed | Duty on tag + MCR-015/054 |
| Communication | Night shift undoes tie | Shift handover + tag |

---

## 3. Ergonomics of erect / dismantle [SYNTHESIS]

- Manual handling of tubes/boards — team lifts, gin wheel only within MCR-031  
- Avoid over-reach outside guardrails  
- Work platforms complete before loading materials  
- Heat stress: rest/water regimes per Anabeeb heat SWP when linked (GAP-020)  
- Night work: lighting of access and edge  

PPE remains **last** line (MCR-037); collective protection first (MCR-030).

---

## 4. Production pressure

Supervisors and clients share responsibility when schedule pressure drives incomplete platforms. Encyclopedia rule: **tag status is not negotiable** (MCR-028/029). If the job needs incomplete structure, it is a **designed temporary condition** with control measures — not an informal shortcut.
